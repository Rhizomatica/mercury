1/* datalink_arq/carousel.h -- the carousel data plane: erasure-coded rounds
2 *
3 * One connected session moves data both ways. Per direction the RECEIVER
4 * chooses: what each block still needs, and the next round's mode and size
5 * ("send me up to N frames in mode M"). The receiver chooses the mode because
6 * it is the side whose payload decoder must be bound to it in advance, and the
7 * side that sees what arrives.
8 *
9 * Who keys, and when (docs/CAROUSEL-TURNS.md, proved in specs/turns): the
10 * caller is the session's only timing master (M). It alone keys on timers of
11 * its own, and only where the callee (S) cannot be on the air. S keys only in
12 * slots anchored to the end of an M keydown it decoded. M carries both
13 * directions: it sends its own rounds, which S answers with its poll; and it
14 * polls S for S's rounds, which S sends in its slots.
15 *
16 * Data is cut into blocks of up to CAR_MAX_K pieces of CAR_PIECE bytes,
17 * Reed-Solomon coded (rs_erasure.h): any K pieces rebuild a block, so a poll
18 * says how many pieces a block needs, never which. Pieces fit every mode, so
19 * a block is never re-encoded when the mode changes.
20 *
21 * At the MFSK floor the control mode no longer gets through, so a round is
22 * answered with a pattern instead (car_io_t.pattern): ACK "keep going", BREAK
23 * "the block you are on is delivered". There silence also means "keep going":
24 * M continues once S's slot is over, S in its continuation slots.
25 *
26 * This module is the protocol only: no threads, no clock of its own, no I/O
27 * but the callbacks. tests/sim/carousel_bench.c runs two of them on the sim
28 * channel; the ARQ runtime runs one against the modem.
29 *
30 * Copyright (C) 2026 Rhizomatica
31 * SPDX-License-Identifier: GPL-3.0-or-later
32 */
